# CakeBazar Founder Reset v1

**Status:** Accepted strategic baseline  
**Owner:** Founder

**Persian companion:** [FOUNDER_RESET.fa.md](FOUNDER_RESET.fa.md)

## Executive decision

CakeBazar will not begin by building a full cake marketplace.

It will begin as a **managed, trust-first request network** for a narrow custom-cake use case. The first product is a concierge workflow that helps a customer create a complete brief, reaches a curated group of sellers, collects comparable responses, and follows the selected order to a verified outcome.

The company earns the right to automate only after the manual workflow demonstrates repeatable demand, supply responsiveness, fulfillment quality, and a plausible economic model.

## Evidence-based diagnosis

### Valuable assets already created

1. **A strong emotional insight**  
   Customers are protecting a celebration, memory, relationship, and social moment — not merely purchasing baked goods.

2. **A differentiated strategic direction**  
   Trust, seller quality, reliability, and recovery support are more defensible than competing only on catalog size or price.

3. **A substantial body of structured thinking**  
   The repository contains product, operations, finance, architecture, investor, and innovation material that can be reused after consolidation.

4. **A functioning visual prototype**  
   The current Next.js application proves a brand and design direction can be implemented.

5. **Correct warnings inside the existing strategy**  
   Existing documents already warn against premature AI, early geographic expansion, weak seller quality, and feature sprawl.

### Critical weaknesses

1. **Documentation maturity is being mistaken for startup maturity**  
   Many pages describe future systems, but there are no verified marketplace transactions, completed research records, or repeatable operating data.

2. **The product is currently a static presentation**  
   Search, sign-up, seller discovery, order requests, verification, payments, reviews, admin operations, and analytics are not functional.

3. **The MVP is over-scoped**  
   Reliability scores, a celebration guarantee, reverse marketplace, order management, reviews, and support are all treated as initial requirements even though several require transaction history, seller density, legal rules, and operational staffing.

4. **Financial models are unsupported hypotheses**  
   Average order value, commission, customer acquisition cost, repeat rate, support cost, seller productivity, growth, and valuation have not been grounded in current evidence.

5. **Investor language is ahead of proof**  
   Market leadership, network effects, data moat, large-market claims, and multi-revenue defensibility are future possibilities, not current assets.

6. **No operating cadence exists**  
   There are no active issues, pull requests, experiment owners, real interview records, or weekly evidence reviews.

## Reframed customer problem

The initial customer problem is not:

> “There is no website to order cake.”

The stronger hypothesis is:

> “For an important custom-cake occasion, customers spend too much time translating an emotional idea into a feasible order, comparing uncertain sellers, and worrying about quality, timing, communication, and delivery.”

This problem must be tested separately in Iran and Germany. It must not be assumed to have the same intensity, user behavior, or solution in both markets.

## Reframed seller problem

The initial seller problem is not simply dependence on Instagram.

The stronger hypothesis is:

> “Capable small cake creators lose time on incomplete requests, low-intent inquiries, repetitive communication, uncertain customer expectations, and fragmented reputation — but may resist a platform that takes commission, controls customer relationships, or adds operational burden.”

The seller value proposition must therefore be proven before asking for exclusivity, commission, complex onboarding, or deep verification.

## Phase 0 product: CakeBazar Concierge

### Customer promise

“Tell us the occasion, date, location, budget, servings, style, dietary constraints, and inspiration. We help you turn it into a complete brief and find suitable available creators.”

### Seller promise

“Receive clearer, better-qualified requests that fit your location, capacity, style, and price range. Respond quickly without repeating the same discovery conversation.”

### Manual operating flow

1. Customer submits a structured brief.
2. An operator checks missing information and feasibility.
3. The request is sent to a small relevant seller pool.
4. Sellers respond with availability, price range, conditions, and portfolio references.
5. The operator normalizes responses for fair comparison.
6. Customer selects a seller.
7. Payment and contracting are initially handled only through a legally reviewed pilot method.
8. CakeBazar records milestones and follows up after the celebration.
9. Both parties provide structured outcome feedback.

### Why manual first

Manual operation exposes the real taxonomy, edge cases, trust needs, seller objections, failure modes, response times, and support cost before they are frozen into software.

## Trust architecture by maturity

### Level 0 — Transparent profiles

- Real contact ownership check
- Portfolio ownership confirmation
- Location and service radius
- Price-range transparency
- Capacity and lead-time statement
- Ingredients/allergen and legal information relevant to launch market

### Level 1 — Manual verified outcome badges

After real completed orders:

- Completed through pilot
- Responds within a measured range
- Delivered on agreed date
- Customer confirmed outcome

### Level 2 — Reliability indicators

Only after enough verified transactions and a defined minimum sample size:

- Completion rate
- On-time rate
- Response-time range
- Complaint and recovery outcomes

### Level 3 — Controlled support promise

Only after sufficient seller density and written operating rules:

- Escalation support
- Backup-seller attempt
- Defined eligibility and compensation limits

Do not publish a single opaque reliability score or broad “celebration guarantee” in Phase 0.

## Market strategy: Iran and Germany

### Decision

Run discovery in both markets; launch the transactional pilot in only one market and one city.

### Iran discovery hypothesis

Potential advantages to test:

- Faster founder-network access to home sellers and customers
- Strong social-commerce behavior and high visual discovery through social channels
- Potentially faster manual experimentation

Risks to test:

- Trust in a new intermediary
- Seller reluctance to pay commission or move customer relationships
- Payment, refund, delivery, and platform-operating constraints
- Informal business practices and uneven food-safety evidence
- Macroeconomic and currency volatility

### Germany discovery hypothesis

Potential advantages to test:

- Customers may value structured requirements, transparent pricing, documented quality, and reliability
- Higher willingness to pay for premium custom work may exist in selected segments
- Stronger potential for standardized operations and regional expansion after proof

Risks and gates:

- Food businesses generally face registration, hygiene, traceability, allergen, and official-control obligations
- Depending on the operating model, craft-law and professional qualification requirements may be relevant
- Consumer protection, platform liability, privacy, payments, and tax rules require review
- Compliance friction may reduce the addressable home-seller pool

Germany cannot be treated as “the same product translated into German.” A legal and operating model review is a launch gate.

### Market selection scorecard

Score each candidate city from 1 to 5 on:

| Factor | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Accessible qualified seller supply | 20% |
| Customer pain and urgency | 15% |
| Request-to-quote response | 15% |
| Fulfillment and delivery feasibility | 10% |
| Legal and food-safety readiness | 15% |
| Payment and refund feasibility | 10% |
| Founder operating access | 10% |
| Expected acquisition efficiency | 5% |

No launch decision is made before at least 10 customer conversations and 10 seller conversations per candidate market, plus a small live request test where legally appropriate.

## What not to build now

Paused until evidence unlocks them:

- Public open marketplace with hundreds of listings
- AI cake recommender
- Dynamic pricing
- AI trust scoring
- Corporate celebration SaaS
- Supplier marketplace
- Academy and certification
- CakeCoins or loyalty currency
- BNPL and financing
- National delivery network
- Full event operating system
- Investor fundraising narrative based on projected ecosystem revenue

## Phase 0 North Star

**Verified successful celebration orders originated through CakeBazar.**

A “verified successful celebration order” requires:

- A real customer request
- A real seller response
- Agreed scope and date
- Confirmed fulfillment outcome
- Post-event feedback from at least the customer

Traffic, followers, registered sellers, and page views are supporting signals, not the North Star.

## Evidence gates before building the marketplace MVP

Thresholds are hypotheses and will be revised after the first 10 live requests.

Minimum evidence to proceed:

- At least 20 qualified sellers contacted
- At least 10 sellers willing to participate in a pilot
- At least 30 real customer requests collected
- At least 60% of qualified requests receive one seller response within the agreed service window
- At least 40% receive two or more comparable seller responses
- At least 20% of qualified requests become a confirmed order, or a clear evidence-based explanation shows why a different threshold is appropriate
- At least 90% of confirmed pilot orders are fulfilled on the agreed date
- Qualitative evidence that customers prefer the structured/matched experience to their prior method
- Measured operator time and support cost per request
- A launch-market legal/compliance checklist reviewed by an appropriate professional or authority

Failure to meet a gate is learning, not a reason to hide the result.

## Business model during validation

Do not optimize commission before proving value.

Test in this order:

1. Free pilot to measure behavior and willingness.
2. Seller success fee only after a completed order.
3. Compare seller-paid, customer-paid, and hybrid service-fee concepts through interviews and controlled tests.
4. Measure contribution economics using real operator time, payment fees, support, refunds, recovery cost, and acquisition cost.

No sponsored ranking may override trust or relevance.

## Technology strategy

### Now

- Mobile-first request form
- Simple seller records
- Manual matching dashboard or operations table
- Event instrumentation
- Consent and privacy basics
- Admin visibility into every request

### Later, after evidence

- Customer and seller accounts
- Self-service seller profiles and listings
- Quote workflow
- Order status
- Reviews tied to verified outcomes
- Search and ranking

### AI placement

Use AI first where it reduces internal work without making high-stakes trust decisions:

- Detect missing information in a request
- Normalize customer briefs
- Suggest categories and tags
- Improve seller listing text
- Summarize feedback for operators
- Assist moderation with human review

AI does not approve sellers, calculate public trust scores, set final prices, or promise successful outcomes during Phase 0.

## Operating model

Every week:

1. Review evidence, not document volume.
2. Update the experiment board.
3. Identify the single largest constraint.
4. Decide what to stop.
5. Assign small implementation tasks to Codex only when they support an active experiment or remove a verified bottleneck.
6. Review every pull request against product scope, privacy, trust, localization, tests, and operational simplicity.

## Founder doctrine

- Start narrow enough to learn quickly.
- Treat seller quality as a scarce asset.
- Earn trust with transparent operations before turning it into a score.
- Never use AI to decorate an unvalidated business model.
- Do not fund complexity with imagined future revenue.
- Keep Iran and Germany as hypotheses until evidence selects the first market.
- Build the smallest system that creates one successful celebration repeatedly.
