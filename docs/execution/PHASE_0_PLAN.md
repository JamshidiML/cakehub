# CakeBazar Phase 0 Validation Plan

**Status:** Proposed for execution  
**Owner:** Founder  
**Duration:** 6 weeks  
**Start:** 2026-07-10  
**Review:** Weekly evidence review  
**Persian companion:** [PHASE_0_PLAN.fa.md](PHASE_0_PLAN.fa.md)

## Objective

Select one launch market and prove or disprove that a managed CakeBazar request flow creates enough value for customers and sellers to justify building a functional marketplace MVP.

## Non-goals

Phase 0 is not intended to:

- Launch nationally
- Reach 1,000 orders
- Build a full marketplace
- Raise investment
- Prove every future revenue stream
- Build public AI features
- Automate seller scoring
- Promise guaranteed celebration outcomes

## Core hypotheses

### H1 — Customer pain

Customers with an important custom-cake need experience meaningful friction in specifying the request, finding available qualified sellers, comparing offers, and reducing fulfillment risk.

### H2 — Structured brief value

A guided brief improves request completeness and reduces clarification effort for customer and seller.

### H3 — Seller participation

Qualified sellers respond to well-matched requests because lead quality and reduced communication effort outweigh platform friction.

### H4 — Managed matching value

Customers value curated, comparable responses more than a raw catalog of sellers.

### H5 — Transaction potential

A meaningful share of qualified requests can become confirmed orders without unsustainable operator effort.

### H6 — Trust value

Transparent verification and outcome-linked evidence increase confidence without requiring an opaque score or unconditional guarantee.

### H7 — Launch-market viability

At least one candidate city in Iran or Germany combines sufficient seller supply, customer demand, legal readiness, payment feasibility, and founder operating access.

## Workstreams

### A. Evidence system

Deliverables:

- Research participant log
- Customer interview record
- Seller interview record
- Assumption register
- Live request experiment log
- Weekly learning report
- Market selection scorecard

Rule: no aggregate conclusion without traceable raw evidence.

### B. Customer discovery

Target:

- Minimum 10 interviews in each candidate market
- Include recent buyers, active planners, and people who abandoned or struggled with a custom-cake search
- Prioritize people with an event in the next 60 days for live tests

Topics:

- Last real purchase journey
- Trigger and urgency
- Search channels
- Time spent
- Trust signals
- Failure fears
- Budget and comparison behavior
- Delivery expectations
- Dietary and allergen needs
- Willingness to use managed matching
- Willingness to pay and preferred fee model

Avoid pitching the full company vision before understanding current behavior.

### C. Seller discovery

Target:

- Minimum 10 interviews in each candidate market
- Minimum 20 qualified sellers contacted in the leading market

Topics:

- Current acquisition channels
- Inquiry quality
- Time spent clarifying requests
- Capacity and lead time
- Pricing method
- Delivery model
- Cancellation and complaint experience
- Verification concerns
- Commission sensitivity
- Customer ownership concerns
- Information needed before quoting
- Legal and food-safety readiness

### D. Live concierge test

Use the smallest legally and operationally safe setup.

Customer request fields:

- Occasion
- Date and required time
- City/postcode or service area
- Number of servings
- Budget range
- Cake type and style
- Inspiration images
- Flavor preferences
- Dietary restrictions and allergens
- Delivery or pickup
- Contact and consent

Seller response fields:

- Available: yes/no
- Estimated price or price range
- Confirmation deadline
- Included and excluded items
- Delivery or pickup conditions
- Relevant portfolio examples
- Clarifying questions

Operator records:

- Time to qualify request
- Sellers contacted
- Time to first response
- Number of responses
- Time spent normalizing responses
- Customer decision
- Order outcome
- Support events
- Failure and recovery events
- Customer and seller feedback

## Six-week schedule

### Week 1 — Reset and recruitment

Goals:

- Finalize candidate cities and legal-discovery questions
- Create research and experiment templates
- Recruit first customer and seller interview participants
- Build the market scorecard
- Prepare a simple manual operations stack

Exit criteria:

- At least 5 customer interviews booked per market
- At least 5 seller interviews booked per market
- Evidence repository and IDs ready

### Week 2 — Problem discovery

Goals:

- Complete first interview wave
- Map current customer journeys
- Map seller inquiry workflow
- Rank the highest-frequency friction points
- Revise structured request fields

Exit criteria:

- At least 10 customer interviews completed in total
- At least 10 seller interviews completed in total
- Top five customer and seller pains ranked by evidence count and severity

### Week 3 — Market comparison and prototype

Goals:

- Complete minimum interviews in each market
- Score candidate launch cities
- Build a testable mobile-first request prototype
- Recruit the first curated seller pool
- Define pilot support hours and escalation owner

Exit criteria:

- Market selection decision recorded
- At least 10 pilot-willing sellers in the selected market, or a documented pivot
- Request prototype ready for real users

### Week 4 — First live requests

Goals:

- Run 10 real requests
- Measure request completeness, response rates, time, and operator effort
- Observe customer comprehension and seller objections

Exit criteria:

- Every request has a complete traceable record
- First-response and multi-response rates calculated
- Major failure modes documented

### Week 5 — Refine and repeat

Goals:

- Improve the request flow and matching rules
- Run 10–15 additional real requests
- Test one narrow trust element such as transparent verification or a verified-pilot badge
- Test fee concepts without forcing monetization prematurely

Exit criteria:

- At least 20 cumulative real requests
- Evidence of whether the second iteration improves response and conversion
- Measured operator time per request

### Week 6 — Decision sprint

Goals:

- Reach at least 30 cumulative real requests where feasible
- Analyze the full funnel and qualitative evidence
- Update unit economics using real operator time and observed costs
- Decide Build, Iterate, Pivot, or Stop

Exit criteria:

- Phase 0 evidence report
- Market decision
- Product decision
- Updated assumptions
- Approved or rejected marketplace MVP scope

## Core metrics

### Acquisition and demand

- Qualified customer requests
- Source of request
- Cost per qualified request
- Percentage with event date inside the target window

### Request quality

- Completion rate without operator follow-up
- Average missing fields
- Operator qualification time

### Marketplace response

- Percentage receiving at least one seller response
- Percentage receiving at least two comparable responses
- Median time to first response
- Seller response rate per matched request

### Conversion

- Quote-view rate
- Customer selection rate
- Confirmed order rate
- Main non-conversion reasons

### Fulfillment and trust

- On-time fulfillment rate
- Scope-change rate
- Complaint rate
- Recovery rate
- Customer outcome rating
- Seller experience rating

### Economics

- Operator minutes per request
- Support minutes per confirmed order
- Real payment and transaction costs
- Acquisition cost
- Refund and recovery cost
- Contribution-margin scenario using observed costs

## Initial decision thresholds

These are provisional hypotheses, not facts.

| Signal | Initial threshold |
|---|---:|
| Pilot-willing qualified sellers | >= 10 |
| Qualified requests | >= 30 |
| At least one response in service window | >= 60% |
| At least two comparable responses | >= 40% |
| Qualified request to confirmed order | >= 20% |
| Confirmed orders fulfilled on agreed date | >= 90% |
| Median customer outcome rating | >= 4/5 |
| Operator time | Measured and economically explainable |

Thresholds may be changed only with a written evidence-based rationale.

## Weekly evidence review

Every week answer:

1. What did we observe?
2. Which assumption became stronger?
3. Which assumption became weaker?
4. What surprised us?
5. What is the current largest constraint?
6. What will we stop doing?
7. What is the smallest next experiment?
8. Does any software task now have enough evidence to justify implementation?

## Decision outcomes

### Build

Proceed to functional marketplace MVP only if demand, supply response, fulfillment, economics, and legal readiness are credible.

### Iterate

Continue manual operation when value exists but the funnel or economics are not stable.

### Pivot

Change the customer segment, occasion type, city, request model, seller type, or value proposition when evidence contradicts the initial wedge.

### Stop

Pause the marketplace concept when customer pain is weak, seller participation is poor, fulfillment risk is unacceptable, or economics cannot plausibly work.

## Definition of done

Phase 0 is complete when the founder can make a traceable decision from real evidence — not when the website looks finished.
